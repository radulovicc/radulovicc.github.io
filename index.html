About

I'm a third-year Computer and Control Engineering student at the Faculty of Technical Sciences in Novi Sad, working where machine learning meets control theory. What I care about is getting real robots to learn how to act — on hardware, not just in simulation.

My focus began with reinforcement learning for continuous control, sparked by building RL agents for autonomous-driving challenges and deepened through model-based RL. Reproducing TD-MPC from scratch for the EEML summer school was a turning point: the researchers I met there pointed me toward generative models, and I've since been drawn to diffusion and flow matching as a principled foundation for learning robot policies — the same family of methods behind modern Vision-Language-Action (VLA) systems.

Today I run a robot-learning research project at the Institute for Artificial Intelligence in Novi Sad, working hands-on with a real FANUC arm: building the data pipeline, moving the workcell into simulation, and fine-tuning and evaluating VLA policies.

My ambition is to become a researcher in Embodied AI and Robot Learning and to pursue a master's and PhD at a top robotics group. I learn fastest with strong mentorship and hard problems — and that's exactly what I'm looking for.

Vision-Language-Action learning on a FANUC arm

2026 — present
Institute for Artificial Intelligence, Novi Sad — Research Intern → Continuing Researcher

My main research: an end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) system on a real FANUC CRX collaborative arm. The robot looks at a scene, reads a natural-language instruction, and produces the motion to carry it out. I own the whole path — from machining the hardware, through collecting data and moving the setup into simulation, to training and evaluating the policy on the real robot.

EEML Summer School & the TD-MPC reproduction

2026
Eastern European Machine Learning Summer School — poster session

EEML 2026 is where my research direction clicked. Alongside lectures and hands-on sessions from leading machine-learning researchers, I reproduced TD-MPC from scratch for the poster session — and the conversations I had there, with researchers from Google DeepMind, pushed me toward the generative models and VLA systems I work on now. The poster went on to win Best Poster among ~250 participants, most of them MSc and PhD students.

▸ The reproduction — for the curious
1

What TD-MPC is

A model-based RL method that learns a latent world model and plans in it — bridging reinforcement learning and model-predictive control, which is exactly the overlap of my degree.

2

Built from scratch

Re-implemented the method in a clean, CleanRL-style codebase to understand every moving part rather than running someone else's repo.

3

The trained agent in action

The reproduced agent learns to run the HalfCheetah locomotion benchmark end-to-end from its learned latent world model.

4

Ablation on the latent-consistency loss

Studied how the consistency term shapes training — trading some stability for a faster, higher-peaking policy.

5

The poster

Presented the work at the poster session and received the Best Poster award.
